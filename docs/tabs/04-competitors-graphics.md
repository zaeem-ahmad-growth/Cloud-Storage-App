### Cloud Storage: Secure Vault

“Cloud Storage: Secure Vault” · 500+ downloads · rating Not returned · 3 portrait and 14 landscape screenshots

Open listing on Google Play ↗

#### Icon

A white three-lobe cloud sits on a bright blue gradient with soft wave shapes, with a circular badge of down and up arrows across its lower edge. The badge reads as sync rather than capacity, and the icon carries no capacity text or promotional badge. Ten of the eleven competitors place a capacity or offer badge on the icon; this one does not, which is what the Free 100 GB study recommends.

#### Feature graphic

The app name is set in two lines at the left over a pale blue wave field, with a phone mock showing a Your vault home and a 25 GB of 100 GB storage ring at the right, plus floating photo and video cards, the line Backup & Restore across multiple devices, and a solid blue Free 100 GB button. The promotional Free 100 GB wording runs against the guidance in this tab, which advises keeping free-storage offers off the feature graphic.

#### Screenshot system

Seven phone panels run headline-above-device on one pale blue wave background, mixing three portrait panels with four landscape ones. The sequence covers upload, WhatsApp media backup, video offload, photo security, encrypted file storage and a three-card plan panel. Two further sets of five repeat the same designs at 1280 and 2560 pixels wide and drop the plan panel.

#### Design assessment

The most internally consistent set in this library: one background system, one type treatment, and real interface in every panel rather than decorative mockups. Three things to watch. Panels 2 and 7 build their message on WhatsApp media and name that brand in the artwork, which is worth a separate listing-policy check even though competitor names are fine in research. The Files panel shows a folder named snaptube, another third-party app name, inside an otherwise generic mock file list. The plan panel promises Free 100 GB on sign-in, which agrees with the current full description but still has to match the tier the app actually grants.

<a id="g-patterns"></a>

Across all 11 competitors

## Cross-app design patterns

Icon silhouette

Large filled clouds survive reduction better than thin strokes and several nested badge details. Preserve a recognizable outer shape and test internal arrows or locks at small sizes. Nova's material depth is visually distinctive, while Daily Utility Apps shows the simplest upload construction. References: [06](#g-06-cloud-storage-backup-drive) [11](#g-11-nova-cloud).

Badge alignment

Fazcon and ANZ place their circular symbols along the lower cloud edge; MAPIDIRECTIONS places its ring inside the cloud. These create different visual balances. For the approved four-lobe concept, keep the badge on the cloud's intended centerline and verify perceived weight as well as coordinates. References: [02](#g-02-fazcon) [03](#g-03-anz-cloud-drive) [04](#g-04-cloud-backup).

Color system

Blue is the category norm across the sample, so it is a familiarity cue rather than a differentiator. White UI, one consistent accent and a distinctive silhouette offer clearer ownership than adding more gradients. Nova provides a useful purple/cyan exception; MindByte provides a quieter white-surface treatment. References: [07](#g-07-cloud-storage-drive-backup) [11](#g-11-nova-cloud).

Portrait composition

Most sets place a short heading above one tall interface. Gallery images or controls occasionally extend beyond the phone. Stable heading position, phone size and surrounding space make a sequence easier to scan than panels that repeatedly switch composition. References: [01](#g-01-cloudgate) [02](#g-02-fazcon) [07](#g-07-cloud-storage-drive-backup) [10](#g-10-cloud-storage).

Landscape adaptation

Fazcon and Fuzon redistribute phone, headline and supporting objects across the width. This is a composition change, not a crop of a portrait. The captured landscapes usually show a portrait phone within a wide marketing canvas; they do not establish native tablet or landscape app support. References: [02](#g-02-fazcon) [05](#g-05-cloud-storage-drive).

Connected popups

Fuzon's category chooser, Daily Utility Apps' completion dialog and MindByte's share-link control retain visible source context. This is the most useful recurring treatment for the requested popup behavior. Enlarge the relevant element only enough to make its function readable. References: [05](#g-05-cloud-storage-drive) [06](#g-06-cloud-storage-backup-drive) [07](#g-07-cloud-storage-drive-backup).

Message hierarchy

Capacity, backup, restore and media are the recurring opening hooks. Vaults, document scanning, sharing and AI organization appear later. Capacity statements vary between assets on several listings; a future set should distinguish available allowance from paid maximum capacity. References: [01](#g-01-cloudgate) [04](#g-04-cloud-backup) [07](#g-07-cloud-storage-drive-backup).

Consistency

DataHatch and Utility Forge publish visibly different screenshot families. These may reflect device sets or updates; no experiment history is available. A new set should deliberately preserve its palette, typography, device proportions and callout treatment across orientations. References: [08](#g-08-datahatch) [09](#g-09-cloud-storage-sync).

<a id="g-guidance"></a>

For your next graphics set

## Guidance for future graphics

Keep the approved icon identity

Use the latest approved four-circle cloud as the source. Carry forward only authorized adjustments: center circular arrow/lock badges, remove unwanted 3D frame outlines, keep the 2D cloud silhouette consistent, match background shades within the 2D and 3D groups, and keep the area outside rounded corners white in comparison presentations. These are recorded preferences, not changes performed by this research.

Choose one screenshot system

For a calm productivity direction, start with MindByte's hierarchy: a short dark heading, a large real screen, a restrained blue emphasis and one connected callout. If stronger category color is preferred, use Fazcon's consistent portrait structure. Do not mix two heading systems within one approved sequence.

Build a six-screen narrative

A practical sequence is: core backup value and home screen; photo/video preservation; visible backup progress and completion; restore selection; private files or vault if implemented; and document scanning or link sharing if implemented. Use actual app screens, real feature names and verified storage terms.

Design landscape separately

For each selected portrait message, create a wide composition with the screen on one side and a short benefit on the other. Keep the same colors, source UI and feature-associated popup. Do not rotate the phone UI or imply a tablet interface that the app does not have.

Treat the feature graphic as its own asset

Use one clear benefit and a restrained visual narrative at 1024 x 500. It should complement the icon rather than become an enlarged duplicate. The earlier rulebook's 1920 x 1080 size can be used for a custom landscape marketing image; it is not the Google Play feature-graphic upload size.

Evaluate at realistic sizes

Before selecting an icon, compare the exact assets at 32, 48, 64 and 96 pixels on a white surface. These are proposed inspection sizes, not guaranteed Play Store layout dimensions. Check arrow direction, badge alignment, cloud separation and whether thin frame lines disappear unevenly.

Test one variable at a time

A future comparison can hold the four-lobe cloud fixed while testing 2D versus 3D materials, or hold the material fixed while testing arrows versus lock. For screenshots, hold the message and source screen fixed while testing white versus blue framing. No uplift estimate is supported by this archive.

Use references as design evidence

Study composition, contrast, scale and feature emphasis. Generate new artwork around the app's own UI and approved identity. Competitor storage, security, speed and AI claims are not requirements or verified capabilities of the app being designed.

<a id="g-requirements"></a>

Google Play

## Play Store production requirements

Google Play's current preview-asset guidance specifies a 512 x 512 PNG app icon and a 1024 x 500 JPEG or 24-bit PNG feature graphic. The feature graphic and landscape screenshots are distinct asset roles.

Google recommends keeping feature-graphic focal content away from cutoff zones and avoiding tiny details, prominent icon duplication, device imagery and promotional wording such as free offers. Several references use these treatments; observed competitor practice is not a template for compliance.

Google permits up to eight screenshots per supported device type. More than eight unique images in this archive therefore does not establish that a listing violates that limit. Device-specific screenshots have additional requirements; check the intended upload slot before production.

[Google Play preview asset guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151?hl=en)

Competitor snapshot collected on 14 September 2026 from the 11 supplied public Google Play URLs, and our own listing re-read on 25 September 2026, both requesting English and US listing context. User aliases and package IDs are retained alongside current listing titles and publishers. Original image responses were archived without redesign, cropping or recoloring. Dimensions and orientation were measured from saved files.
