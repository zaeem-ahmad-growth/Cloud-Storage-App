Cloud Storage ASO By Zaeem

Live-scraped competitive research for a new cloud storage / vault / backup app — who ranks on the generic keywords, which keywords a no-brand app can actually win, and the exact listing to ship.

The verdict

Brands (Drive, Photos, Dropbox, TeraBox, MEGA) permanently own ranks #1–3 on every head term — but the top-10 is genuinely winnable. Apps with only 24K–513K installs hold top-10 slots on “cloud storage”, “cloud backup” and “phone backup” today, and the #1 result for “cloud backup” is a 269K-install app released Dec 2024. Position as a generic cloud drive + privacy skin + phone-backup keyword set, launch on the wide-open long tail, and climb the ladder below.

What the 15 Sep refresh adds

  1. Your 11 competitors don’t win head terms. Across 12 countries, only Fazcon reaches a “cloud storage” top-10 (#8 Nigeria, #6 Mexico). Their visibility lives on phrases lifted from their own titles, like “cloud storage cloud drive”, where Fazcon is #1 in all four markets.
  2. Brand-new listings are being boosted. Brown Berry’s “Cloud Storage : Cloud Backup”, released two days ago with 1 install, is already #4 on “cloud space” in the US. Launch with the final listing: the first weeks are when exact-match ranks come cheapest.
  3. Fazcon is opening a second front. Its new, ad-free “Cloud Storage: 1 TB Backup” has 1 install and 12 top-10 placements across the four markets, including “1tb cloud storage” (#4 PK) and “cloud storage premium” (#6 NG).
  4. The 8 Sep title is taken. “Cloud Storage - Photo Backup” already belongs to another app, which reaches a top-10 only for “cloud photos” in Nigeria. The listing section replaces it with titles scored against the board.
  5. Relevance now leads the ranking. Cleaner, contacts and WhatsApp terms drop to the bottom. “Cloud storage backup and restore” tops the board in every market.

Keyword board ·

Keyword opportunity board, relevance first

Priority = relevance² × opportunity. Squaring relevance means an off-niche keyword can’t climb on volume alone. Relevance blends intent tier (55%), how niche the top-10 is (35%) and whether your competitors rank there (10%). Opportunity blends non-brand apps in the top-10, autocomplete demand, the median install proxy and the entry bar. The entry bar is the smallest non-brand app in the top-10.

Demand comes from Play’s own autocomplete: the fewer letters it takes before Play suggests the phrase, the higher the score. The volume proxy is the median installs of the top-10, a relative signal inflated by brand apps. 8 Sep values come from the first run, which used a slightly different brand list.

    Best practices · industry sources 2025–26

    What the industry says

    Title · 30 chars

    Heaviest-weighted text field. Lead with the highest-volume keyword — for a no-brand app, the generic term goes first.

    AppTweak · AppRadar
    Short description · 80 chars

    Indexed, second-strongest field. Use complementary keywords not already in the title, phrased as a conversion message.

    AppTweak
    Long description · 4,000 chars

    Fully indexed — Play has no hidden keyword field. Primary keywords at 2–3% density, front-loaded into the first paragraph, headers and bullet starts; stay at the low end.

    AppTweak · ASOMobile
    Metadata policy (2024+)

    No emoji, ALL CAPS, “#1 / best” claims, or promo text in titles; no keyword stuffing anywhere. All three proposed titles comply.

    Google Play policy
    Beyond text

    Rankings blend keyword relevance with download velocity, listing conversion, retention, review velocity and Android Vitals. Metadata earns impressions; conversion and quality hold the rank.

    MobileAction · AppRadar
    Custom store listings

    Up to ~50 variants by country, language, ads campaign, search keyword and churned users — case studies show up to ~30% CVR lift. Localize IN, BR, ID, MX early.

    Phiture · MobileAction

    Tooling note: industry volume estimates come from AppTweak, Sensor Tower/data.ai, MobileAction and ASOMobile. This research uses no paid tool; it combines live result scraping with Play autocomplete. That is defensible for ranking decisions, but it is not absolute search volume.

    Risks

    Watch-outs

    • “Unlimited storage” claimsWinnable result list, but a misleading-claims magnet: real infrastructure cost, angry reviews when throttled, Play deceptive-behavior exposure. The listing targets the intent honestly.
    • Velocity churnPlay rewards fresh apps hard (Cleanup: Nov 2025 → 28M installs), so these results turn over. Several fast-ranking 2026 backup apps are already burning on reviews; their slots are takeable. Re-scrape monthly.
    • Conversion gates rankHold head terms only with screenshots that show the free-GB offer, backup and vault in the first three, early rating prompts (winners run 4.2–4.8) and clean Android Vitals.
    • No free tier, no rankingEvery winner leads with 5–20GB free; without one the listing converts poorly regardless of keywords. Budget for it.
    • Result intent trapsSkip “my cloud” (WD and camera apps) and “data drive” (data-plan apps). Claim “whatsapp backup” or “contacts backup” only if those features actually ship.
    • Proxy caveatWinnability, demand and volume are one-day snapshots. Re-validate before each metadata change.
    • The new-app boost fades newListings under 60 days old hold top-10 slots with a handful of installs. Those ranks are provisional: install velocity and retention in the first weeks decide whether they stick.
    • Keyword-mash titles newTitles like “Cloud Storage App Drive Backup” win exact matches today but sit close to Play’s repetitive-keyword policy. The recommended titles read as plain phrases with no repeated words.
    • Plan sizes in metadata new“100GB” and “1TB” rank well, but only use them where a real plan exists. A size the product doesn’t offer invites policy strikes and one-star reviews.
    • Ads are the category norm new10 of the 11 competitors carry ads. “No ads” is a real differentiator in the listing, but only if every tier keeps that promise.
    • Web result depth newPlay’s signed-out web search returned 8–30 results per query. Ranks past that depth are invisible here, and on-device results can also be personalized.

    Method, 15 Sep 2026: Google Play web search (English) scraped live in four catalogs (US, PK, AE, NG) for 94 keywords. The keywords are the 34 from the 8 Sep report, 18 mined from the 11 competitors’ metadata, 10 lifted from their titles and 32 niche-relevant Play autocomplete suggestions. That made 376 result lists, 30 deep where Play allowed. Details were fetched for all 254 apps in any top-10 plus the 11 competitors (522 apps seen in total), with 0 fetch failures. Demand comes from Play autocomplete: each keyword is typed out word by word and scored on how early Play suggests it. A 12-country probe covered five head terms.

    Classification: brands are platform owners and established cloud or utility brands, identified by developer (Google, Microsoft, Dropbox, Flextech/TeraBox, MEGA, Amazon, Samsung, pCloud, Proton, Synology, WD, Sync.com, IDrive, Nord Security, Avast, AVG, Norton and similar). Apps are sorted by title into cloud storage/backup (weight 1), device backup (0.5), vault, file manager, gallery and transfer (0.25), and cleaner, recovery and other (0).

    Scores: Relevance = 0.55·intent tier (core 1, adjacent 0.7, peripheral 0.3, off-intent 0) + 0.35·niche share of the top-10 + 0.10·min(1, competitors in results ÷ 3). Opportunity = 0.35·non-brand share of the top-10 + 0.30·demand + 0.15·volume proxy (log-scaled median installs) + 0.20·entry bar (log-scaled, smaller is easier). Priority = 100 · relevance² · opportunity. First run, 8 Sep 2026: Python google-play-scraper, 35 seed queries (US), 304 apps detailed.
